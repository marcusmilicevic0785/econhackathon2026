# NYC access equity: exploratory tract regression

## Question and hypothesis

Is the local supply of selected publicly oriented facilities associated with household income, poverty, and the shares of younger or older residents? The hypotheses are that income has a positive association and poverty/young-resident shares have negative associations. These are hypotheses, not findings assumed by the code. Report confidence intervals even when hypotheses fail.

## Data and access definition

The analysis uses 2019–2023 ACS five-year estimates, 2023 Census tract Gazetteer land areas/internal points, and a frozen NYC Department of City Planning Facilities Database snapshot. It is a current-supply/earlier-demographics comparison, not a historically aligned causal design. Counts within 400, 800, and 1,200 metres of the Census internal point use great-circle distances. Internal points are guaranteed geographic reference points, not population-weighted centroids or front doors. Distances are not pedestrian routes; water, roads, transit, and borough edges can matter substantially.

The exact facility type allow-list is recorded in `outputs/qa/facility_selection.json`. It includes public/branch libraries when identified by those names; community/neighborhood/flagship/state/city-state parks and playgrounds; recreation centers; the exact generic NYCHA community-center type; and senior centers. Specialized/academic libraries and NYCHA administrative, storage, vacant, and other ambiguous facility types are excluded. This deliberately conservative inventory does not represent all third places. Locations may be closed, charge fees, or restrict age/residency; operating access is unverified. The full category distribution is retained for audit.

The main radius measure can cross tract boundaries. Within-tract counts are an auxiliary administrative measure. Deduplication removes same-category locations with identical rounded coordinates (five decimal places, roughly a metre); this can merge distinct co-located facilities. Invalid coordinates are excluded from radius counts and explicitly reported. Source `uid` is unique-checked. FacDB `ct2020` supports auxiliary tract assignment; radius counts do not depend on that field.

## Formula and units

\[
\log(1+A_i)=\alpha+\beta_1\log(MHI_i)+\beta_2 Poverty_i+
\beta_3 Youth_i+\beta_4 Older_i+\beta_5\log(1+Density_i)+\gamma_{borough(i)}+\epsilon_i.
\]

- `A`: selected sites within 800 m, a count; alternatives: 400/1,200 m.
- `MHI`: median household income in 2023 dollars; no averaging of tract medians.
- `Poverty`: percentage below poverty using the poverty-status universe, not total population.
- `Youth`: percentage ages 15–24, summed from the age/sex table.
- `Older`: percentage ages 65+.
- `Density`: population per land square mile.

Shares are 0–100, so a share coefficient corresponds to a one-percentage-point change in that share. `log_income` is on the natural-log dollar scale. Log(1+count) effects must not be reported as changes in the untransformed count without an explicit back-transformation. Each tract receives equal OLS weight; a tract association is not a person-average association.

## Estimation and quality checks

Retain populated tracts (population ≥100) and complete cases, publish exclusion GEOIDs/row counts, reject duplicate keys and unmatched ACS/Gazetteer joins, convert all negative ACS sentinel values to missing, and retain original MOEs in extracts. OLS uses HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust covariance; borough effects absorb borough means. Diagnostics include VIF, rank/condition number, residual scatter/QQ plots, Breusch–Pagan, Pearson correlations/covariances, radius sensitivity, and spatial sensitivity when generated by the pipeline.

ACS margins of error are not propagated into regression uncertainty. Published median income is top-coded in the highest-income tracts; treating the published threshold as numeric understates true variation there. Income and poverty can be strongly collinear; inspect VIF and sensitivity before interpreting independent coefficients. Spatial dependence can make HC3 intervals optimistic. No neighborhood-level causal inference, demand/quality/access equivalence, or person-level inference follows from this design. Separate youth and older-adult policy hypotheses from observed associations.

Spatial sensitivity groups tracts into approximate 2 km geographic tiles (111.2 km per degree latitude, 84.3 km per degree longitude at NYC) with small-sample corrected cluster covariance and t intervals. An eight-nearest-neighbor, row-standardized directed weight matrix supports a residual Moran statistic with 499 seeded permutations and a two-sided test around its randomization expectation. These exploratory diagnostics do not guarantee that the tile size captures all spatial dependence. Main and sensitivity inference should be read together.

Run: `econhack run --model nyc`; inspect `outputs/tables/nyc_equity*` and the model status registry. Source URLs and hashes are in `data/manifest.json`.
